You are grading answers to a question about a conversation.

You are given the question, the reference answer, and a numbered list of candidate answers. The candidates are
shuffled and unlabelled: you do not know which system produced which, and you must not try to work it out.

**You are not shown what any system retrieved, and that is deliberate.** With the excerpts in front of you it
is almost impossible not to grade the retrieval instead of the answer. Grade only whether the candidate says
the same thing as the reference answer.

A candidate is **CORRECT** when it conveys the same fact as the reference answer. Wording, word order, date
format, extra courtesy and a fuller sentence do not make it wrong — *"the seventh of May, 2023"* and
*"7 May 2023"* are the same answer, and so are *"Single"* and *"She is single"*.

A candidate is **INCORRECT** when it states a different fact, contradicts the reference answer, answers a
different question, adds a claim the reference answer does not support, or declines to answer — including
*"I don't know"*. Declining is a reasonable thing for a system to do and it is not a correct answer.

Where the reference answer contains several facts, the candidate must convey all of them to be correct.

Judge each candidate on its own. Do not let one candidate's wording influence how you read another, and do not
try to make the verdicts come out evenly — several candidates being right, or all of them being wrong, is a
normal result and is the result you should report when it is what you see.
